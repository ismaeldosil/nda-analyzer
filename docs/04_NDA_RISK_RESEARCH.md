## 2. Identified Contractual Risks

### 2.1 Break-Up Fees / Termination Fees

**Description**: Contractual provision requiring one party to pay a fixed sum if the transaction terminates under certain circumstances.

**Risk for Buyer (Halmos)**:
- **Reverse break-up fee**: Buyer pays the target if they can't complete the deal
- Target termination fees: typically **3-4%** of deal value (median 3.2-3.4% per Harvard Law studies)
- Reverse break-up fees: typically **4.5-6.5%** of target equity value
- Can be triggered by: lack of financing, missed deadlines, buyer breach

**Severity**: CRITICAL

**Sources**:
- [Harvard Law Forum - Breakup Fees: Picking Your Number](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2012/09/11/breakup-fees-picking-your-number/)
- [Harvard Law Forum - PE Buyer/Public Target M&A Deal Study 2015-17](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2018/10/02/private-equity-buyer-public-target-ma-deal-study-2015-17-review/)
- [Harvard Law Forum - Break Fees and Broken M&A Deals](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2017/11/15/break-fees-and-broken-ma-deals/)
- [Rutgers Law - Reverse Termination Fees in M&A (Academic Study)](https://sites.rutgers.edu/darius-palia/wp-content/uploads/sites/218/2019/07/RTF.pdf)

### 2.2 Standstill Provisions

**Description**: Clause preventing the buyer from taking hostile acquisition actions, purchasing shares, or soliciting proxies during a specified period.

**Risk for Buyer (Halmos)**:
- 6 months to 2 years restriction on acquisition activities
- Can operate as "backdoor standstill" through use clauses
- Limits flexibility to pursue the deal if negotiations fail

**Relevant Legal Case**: *Martin Marietta Materials, Inc. v. Vulcan Materials Co. (Delaware, 2012)* - The court determined that although the NDA had no explicit standstill, the "use" clause operated as a de facto standstill, prohibiting Martin Marietta from pursuing a hostile acquisition for 4 months.

**Severity**: HIGH

**Sources**:
- [DealRoom - Everything You Need to Know About Standstill Agreements](https://dealroom.net/blog/standstill-agreement)
- [Barnes & Thornburg - The NDA Use Clause: Avoiding an Unintentional Standstill](https://btlaw.com/en/insights/alerts/2021/the-nda-use-clause-avoiding-an-unintentional-standstill)
- [Ropes & Gray - Avoiding Pitfalls of "Use" Clauses in NDAs](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2019/11/avoiding-pitfalls-of-use-clauses-in-ndas)
- [Faegre Drinker - M&A 101: Key Concepts in Non-Disclosure Agreements](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2018/3/ma-101-key-concepts-in-nondisclosure-agreements)

### 2.3 Residual Clauses

**Description**: Exempts the recipient from liability for using information retained in an employee's "unaided memory".

**Risk for Seller (but relevant for analysis)**:
- Allows recipient to use memorized information without restriction
- Makes proving NDA breach difficult
- May allow competition based on confidential information

**Why it's relevant for Halmos**: If Halmos is the recipient, a broad residual clause could be beneficial. But if absent or very restrictive, it could limit how they use learned information.

**Relevant Legal Case**: *Space Data Corp. v. Google* (N.D. Cal. 2017) - The court ruled that the plaintiff couldn't demonstrate that Google's conduct "exceeded the scope of the NDA" due to the residual clause. (NOTE: NOT Delaware precedent - persuasive only)

**Severity**: MEDIUM

**Sources**:
- [Venable LLP - Residual Clauses in an NDA for M&A Transactions](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2018/11/residual-clauses-in-an-nda-for-ma-transactions)
- [Dentons - Beware of "Residuals" Clauses in NDAs for M&A Transactions](https://www.dentons.com/en/insights/articles/2018/october/8/beware-of-residuals-clauses-in-ndas-for-m-a-transactions)
- [Justia - Space Data Corp. v. Alphabet Inc., Case 5:16-cv-03260 (N.D. Cal.)](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/california/candce/5:2016cv03260/299750/255/)

### 2.4 Non-Circumvention Clauses

**Description**: Prevents one party from "going around" the other to deal directly with third parties in a business arrangement.

**Risk for Buyer (Halmos)**:
- May prevent direct contact with seller if there's an intermediary
- Creates liability for transaction fees
- Can extend for long periods (24+ months)

**Additional Risk**: In case of circumvention, penalties can be up to 3x the commission or fee the affected party should have received.

**Relevant Legal Case**: *Goodrich Capital LLC v. Vector Capital Corp.* (S.D.N.Y. 2012, Judge Jed S. Rakoff) - The court denied in part Vector's motion to dismiss, finding plausible allegations that Vector attempted to avoid paying an alleged $3.5 million fee in violation of the non-circumvention clause. *(Note: Case settled; fee amount was alleged, not adjudicated.)*

**Severity**: HIGH

**Sources**:
- [CourtListener - Goodrich Capital LLC v. Vector Capital Corp., 1:11-cv-09247 (S.D.N.Y.)](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/4349974/goodrich-capital-llc-v-vector-capital-corporation/)
- [Harvard Law Forum - M&A Confidentiality Agreements](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2012/05/10/delaware-court-issues-guidance-about-ma-confidentiality-agreements/)
- [Faegre Drinker - M&A 101: Key Concepts in NDAs](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2018/3/ma-101-key-concepts-in-nondisclosure-agreements)

### 2.5 Exclusivity / No-Shop Clauses

**Description**: Prevents the seller from negotiating with other potential buyers during a specified period.

**Risk for Buyer (Halmos)**: Generally beneficial, but if absent, the seller can continue looking for other offers during the process.

**Note**: More common in LOIs than NDAs, but may appear in NDAs.

**Severity**: MEDIUM (informative)

**Sources**:
- [Morgan & Westfield - The M&A NDA: A Complete Guide](https://morganandwestfield.com/knowledge/ma-non-disclosure-agreements/)
- [Dealert.AI - NDAs in High-Stakes Deals](https://dealert.ai/blog/p/ndas-in-high-stakes-deals-drafting-templates-for-pe-and-ma-professionals/)

### 2.6 Overly Broad Non-Solicitation

**Description**: Excessive restrictions on hiring the seller's employees.

**Risk for Buyer (Halmos)**:
- May cover ALL employees, not just key executives
- May extend to PE fund's portfolio companies
- May prohibit even non-deal-related hires

**Acceptable standard**: 18-24 months, with exceptions for:
- General solicitations (public ads)
- Non-directed employment agencies
- Employees who contact the buyer on their own initiative
- Employees terminated by the seller

**Severity**: MEDIUM

**Sources**:
- [Outside GC - 5 Highly Negotiated Provisions in Private Equity NDAs](https://www.outsidegc.com/blog/5-highly-negotiated-provisions-in-private-equity-non-disclosure-agreements)
- [Kutak Rock - NDAs in Merger & Acquisition Transactions](https://www.kutakrock.com/newspublications/publications/2023/march/prickly-pear-march-2023-newsletter/ndas-in-merger-acquisition-transactions)

### 2.7 Restrictions on Financing Sources

**Description**: Limits the buyer's ability to communicate with financing sources or form consortiums.

**Risk for Buyer (Halmos)**:
- May prevent obtaining debt financing for the deal
- May prohibit "club deals" with other PE funds
- May create lock-ups with financial institutions

**Common exception**: "Tree system" arrangements where an exclusive team works on financing for one bidder while other teams can work for other bidders.

**Severity**: HIGH (for PE)

**Sources**:
- [Outside GC - 5 Highly Negotiated Provisions in Private Equity NDAs](https://www.outsidegc.com/blog/5-highly-negotiated-provisions-in-private-equity-non-disclosure-agreements)
- [Noerr - Non-disclosure agreements in M&A & PE transactions](https://www.noerr.com/en/insights/non-disclosure-agreements-in-ma-pe-transactions)

---

### 2.8 Portfolio Company Restrictions

**Description**: The NDA applies restrictions to the PE fund's portfolio companies, not just the fund itself.

**Risk for Buyer (Halmos)**:
- Portfolio companies may be limited in their operations
- May be considered "recipients" of confidential information
- May be subject to implicit non-compete

**Necessary protection**: PE Acknowledgement clause (already identified in client requirements).

**Severity**: HIGH (for PE)

**Sources**:
- [Lexology - 5 Highly Negotiated Provisions in Private Equity NDAs](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=3d728acd-8602-42ab-8f63-bd7182c529b9)
- [Noerr - Non-disclosure agreements in M&A & PE transactions](https://www.noerr.com/en/insights/non-disclosure-agreements-in-ma-pe-transactions)

---

### 2.9 Perpetual Confidentiality Obligations

**Description**: Confidentiality obligations with no expiration date.

**Risk for Buyer (Halmos)**:
- Indefinite obligations
- Common in industries like biotech and defense
- May limit buyer's future operations

**Acceptable standard**: 1-3 years for general information, longer for specific trade secrets.

**Severity**: MEDIUM

**Sources**:
- [Dealert.AI - NDAs in High-Stakes Deals](https://dealert.ai/blog/p/ndas-in-high-stakes-deals-drafting-templates-for-pe-and-ma-professionals/)
- [Genesis Law Firm - NDAs in Mergers and Acquisitions](https://www.genesislawfirm.com/nondisclosure-agreements-mergers-acquisitions)

---

### 2.10 Liquidated Damages / Penalties

**Description**: Predetermined fixed amounts for NDA breach.

**Risk for Buyer (Halmos)**:
- Amounts may be disproportionate to actual damage
- May be difficult to negotiate after signing
- May include multiples (e.g., 3x the fee)

**Severity**: HIGH

**Sources**:
- [The M&A Lawyer Blog - What you need to know about M&A confidentiality agreements](https://themalawyer.com/what-you-need-to-know-about-ma-confidentiality-agreements/)

---

### 2.11 Broad Definition of Confidential Information

**Description**: Definition so broad that it's impossible to determine what it covers.

**Risk for Buyer (Halmos)**:
- Everything can be considered "confidential"
- Makes compliance difficult
- Increases risk of inadvertent breach

**Sources**:
- [Genesis Law Firm - NDAs in Mergers and Acquisitions](https://www.genesislawfirm.com/nondisclosure-agreements-mergers-acquisitions)
- [Calkins Law Firm - Protecting Confidentiality in M&A](https://calkinslawfirm.com/protecting-confidentiality-in-ma-legal-best-practices/)

**Severity**: MEDIUM

---

### 2.12 Waiver of Consequential Damages

**Description**: The seller waives the right to claim certain types of damages.

**Risk**: Generally NOT appropriate in NDAs because lost profits and loss of goodwill are the most likely damages from confidentiality breach.

**Severity**: MEDIUM (anomaly if present)

**Sources**:
- [Genesis Law Firm - NDAs in Mergers and Acquisitions](https://www.genesislawfirm.com/nondisclosure-agreements-mergers-acquisitions)

## 4. Relevant Legal Cases

### 4.1 Martin Marietta Materials, Inc. v. Vulcan Materials Co. (Delaware, 2012)

**Relevance**: Demonstrated that "use" clauses can operate as de facto standstill.

**Holding**: The court prohibited Martin Marietta from pursuing a hostile acquisition for 4 months because they violated the NDA's use clause by using confidential information for a transaction not contemplated.

**Lesson**: "Use" clauses must be carefully analyzed, not just explicit standstills.

### 4.2 Space Data Corp. v. Google (N.D. Cal. 2017)

**Relevance**: Demonstrated the difficulty of proving breach when a residual clause exists.

**Holding**: The plaintiff couldn't demonstrate that Google acted outside the NDA's scope due to the existence of a residual clause.

**Lesson**: Residual clauses can be "Trojan horses" that make information protection difficult.

**Note**: NOT Delaware precedent - persuasive authority only.

### 4.3 Goodrich Capital v. Vector Capital (S.D.N.Y. 2012)

**Relevance**: Non-circumvention can create significant liability.

**Holding**: The court found plausible allegations that Vector Capital attempted to avoid paying a $3.5 million fee by violating the non-circumvention clause.

**Lesson**: Non-circumvention clauses can have significant monetary consequences.

## 7. Delaware Law Specific Considerations

> All client sample NDAs use Delaware as governing law. This section analyzes Delaware-specific aspects relevant to the NDA Analyzer.

### 7.1 Key Case: Martin Marietta v. Vulcan Materials (Delaware, 2012)

**Case Facts**:
- Martin Marietta and Vulcan Materials (competitors in aggregates industry) signed NDA and JDA to explore friendly merger
- Martin then launched $5.5B hostile offer using confidential information received under the NDA
- The NDA **had no explicit standstill**

**Key Holdings**:

| Point | Holding |
|-------|---------|
| **"Backdoor Standstill"** | The "use" clause operated as de facto standstill |
| **"Transaction" Definition** | "Between" (not "involving") limited use to negotiated merger |
| **Remedy** | Court imposed **4-month injunction** against hostile offer |

**Key holding from Chancellor Strine's opinion**:

The Court found that even without an explicit standstill provision, other provisions in the confidentiality agreement—particularly restrictive "use" clauses—can operate as an effective "backdoor standstill" obligation. *(Note: This is a paraphrase of the court's reasoning, not a direct quote.)*

**Sources**:
- [Harvard Law - Delaware Court Issues Guidance about M&A Confidentiality Agreements](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2012/05/10/delaware-court-issues-guidance-about-ma-confidentiality-agreements/)
- [Richards Layton & Finger - Martin Marietta Case Analysis](https://www.rlf.com/martin-marietta-materials-inc-v-vulcan-materials-co-delaware-supreme-court-affirms-four-month-delay-of-hostile-exchange-offer-based-on-violations-of-confidentiality-agreements/)

### 7.3 Delaware Uniform Trade Secrets Act (DUTSA)

**Statute**: 6 Del. C. § 2001 et seq.

**Trade Secret Definition**:
- Information with "independent economic value" from not being generally known
- Subject to "reasonable efforts to maintain secrecy"

**Remedies Available under DUTSA**:

| Remedy | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Injunctive Relief** | Prohibit use or disclosure |
| **Actual Damages** | Actual losses + unjust enrichment |
| **Exemplary Damages** | Up to **2x damages** if "willful and malicious" |
| **Attorney's Fees** | If claim in bad faith or willful misappropriation |

**Statute of Limitations**: 3 years from discovery or reasonable discovery.

**Source**: [Delaware Code Title 6 Chapter 20 - Trade Secrets](https://delcode.delaware.gov/title6/c020/)

---

### 7.4 Irreparable Harm and Injunctive Relief in Delaware

Delaware has a favorable standard for obtaining injunctions for NDA breach:

> "Contractual stipulations as to irreparable harm alone suffice to establish that element for the purpose of issuing injunctive relief."

**Practical implications**:

| If the NDA has... | Then... |
|-------------------|---------|
| "Irreparable harm" clause | Easier to obtain injunction |
| "Specific performance" clause | Delaware enforces it strictly |
| "Money damages inadequate" stipulation | Court respects it without questioning |

**Recommendation for NDA Analyzer**: Detect presence of irreparable harm/equitable relief clauses as a **positive indicator** of protection.

---

### 7.5 Forum Selection and Court of Chancery

**"Crystalline Language" Requirement**:
- Clause must be clear to confer exclusive jurisdiction
- If ambiguous, court will NOT interpret as exclusive

**Important Limitation**:
- Court of Chancery **does NOT have jurisdiction** over purely legal claims (only equitable)
- Clause must include fallback to Superior Court or US District Court

**Example of robust clause** (detect if present):

```
"The Court of Chancery of the State of Delaware (or, if the Court of
Chancery does not have jurisdiction, another State court in Delaware
or the federal district court for the District of Delaware) shall be
the sole and exclusive forum."
```

**Flag if**: Forum selection only mentions Court of Chancery without fallback.

**Source**: [Morris Nichols - Forum Selection Provisions: Choose Your Own Adventure](https://www.morrisnichols.com/insights-forum-selection-provisions-choose-your-own-adventure)

## 8. Risk Analysis: Buyer-Friendly vs Seller-Friendly

> **Standard M&A Terminology**: Clauses are classified as **Buyer-Friendly** (favors the buyer), **Seller-Friendly** (favors the seller), **Market Standard** (neutral), or **Balanced** (equitable).

### 8.1 The Client's 6 Core Clauses

| # | Clause | Client Criteria | Risk for BUYER if... | Risk for SELLER if... |
|---|--------|-----------------|----------------------|----------------------|
| 1 | **Governing Law** | Must be Delaware | NOT Delaware (less predictability, less M&A jurisprudence) | NOT Delaware (same risks - it's **neutral**) |
| 2 | **Term** | ≤ 2 years | **> 2 years** (prolonged obligations, limits future operations, memorized info still restricted) | < 1 year (information can be used too soon post-failed deal) |

| 6 | **PE Acknowledgement** | Desirable to exist | **DOESN'T exist** (portfolio companies restricted, can't invest in competitors, limits PE core business) | Exists without limits (buyer can freely invest in direct competition) |
